How it works

On 15 Oct 20261, at SpaceX's test site in McGregor, Texas1, a Roadster drove up a ramp that curls over until the car is upside down. It hung there, dropped off the end, rolled over in mid-air and landed on its wheels. Fans under the floor suck it onto the road, and jets of compressed air push it.

Suction

Under the floor are fans, and skirts that close the gap between the car and the road. The fans pump air out from under the car. With less air underneath, the air above pushes the car down harder than the air below pushes it up.

On the ramp, the car's downforce readout said 4,700 kg2. Spread over a floor of about 4 m² (our estimate), that is only about 11% less pressure under the car than around it, roughly the drop in air pressure between sea level and a hilltop 1 km up.

The push comes through the car's own floor, so it works on a wall or a ceiling as well as on a road. On the ramp it is about twice the car's weight, enough to hold the car there upside down. It also presses the tires harder onto the road, so they grip better.

Air tanks

Nothing burns. The car carries air, mostly nitrogen, squeezed into carbon-fibre tanks. The Rocket version has 600 L2 of tanks holding about 250 kg2 of air at 5,500 psi2, about 375 times normal air pressure.

When a valve opens, the air rushes out through a nozzle and pushes the car the other way, like a balloon you let go of. The Rocket has 102 nozzles: four at the back push it forward, four underneath lift it, and two at the nose turn it.

Nozzles

Each nozzle narrows to a throat and then flares out like a bell. In the throat the air reaches the speed of sound, and in the bell it speeds up further, to about 682 m/s in our model. The four rear nozzles together push with over 80 kN4. That force doesn't depend on tire grip, so it adds to whatever the tires can do.

The valves are either fully open or fully shut. For half power, a valve opens and shuts many times a second and stays open half of the time.

The jump

  1. The car reaches the ramp at about 60 km/h and climbs at about 30 km/h (the speeds our simulation uses). Suction and the curve press it onto the surface.
  2. At the top, about 21 m2 up, it hangs upside down, held by the suction.
  3. At the end of the ramp the skirts lose their seal. The suction is gone in a fraction of a second and the car drops.
  4. The lift nozzles on one side fire and roll the car over. Half a turn takes about half a second.
  5. Just before touchdown all four lift nozzles fire to brake the fall. The jets are cold enough to turn moisture in the air into the white cloud. The landing readout showed 3.3 g2.

Limits

A full tank lasts about 2 s3 of full thrust; our model says 2.1 s. That is enough air for a hop, not for flying around. Refilling from a nitrogen supply takes 2 h5 to 6 h5; Elon Musk says the car refills its own tanks5. The Rocket version is invite-only and not approved for public roads6.

Estimates

Numbers with a small reference come from coverage of the reveal on 15 Oct 2026. Some were never published: the car's weight, the floor area, the exact ramp shape and the strength of the lift nozzles. For those the simulator uses our own estimates. Outlets also disagree on some details, such as the nozzle layout. Every number and its status is listed in the project's specs file.
