Tesla Roadster Programming Simulator

A STEM learning project that connects current technology, programming, physics, and interactive simulation.

Group TUFF · November 2026 · Not affiliated with Tesla, Inc.

Why this idea?

A global headline became a classroom question.

The moment

  • On 15 October 2026, Tesla revealed the Roadster with SpaceX thrusters.
  • The flying-car jump was covered worldwide.
  • It sits at the frontier of technology and public attention.

What we had learned

  • In class we used Scratch and block-based programming.
  • Commands could be stacked like building blocks.
  • Programs made ideas visible and repeatable.

Our idea

  • Turn the news into an interactive simulator.
  • Let students program the car, not just watch it.
  • Connect coding with physics, data, and explanation.

Learning design

Game-based learning with gamification.

Question → Program → Run → Feedback → Improve

Game-based learning

  • The simulator is the learning environment.
  • Students solve a meaningful programming challenge.
  • Learning happens through experimentation and simulation.

Gamification elements

  • Immediate ✓/✗ feedback.
  • Tasks, goals, progress, and reset.
  • Low-risk failure encourages another attempt.

Our verdict

  • This is game-based learning, not pure gamification.
  • The game is the learning environment, not a reward layer.
  • ✓/✗ checks and task goals are borrowed gamification elements.

STEM alignment

Four fields, one simulator.

SScience

  • Motion, force, pressure, gravity.
  • Downforce and thrusters.
  • Observe and explain results.

TTechnology

  • 3D simulation and programming.
  • Real-time telemetry.
  • Data recording and feedback.

EEngineering

  • Design a route and control system.
  • Test, fail, and improve.
  • Balance speed, safety, and control.

MMathematics

  • Speed, distance, time, and units.
  • Angles, coordinates, and conditions.
  • Read data and compare results.

All four are present: Technology is most visible; Engineering and Mathematics are applied through the simulation.

What happens

  • Thrusters roll it level in mid-air.

Why include it

Inside the Tutorial

Three parts guide the learner.

1How to use it

  • Pick a command block.
  • Type its value and insert it.
  • Preview the path, then run.
  • Reset to try again.

2Student tasks

  • Reach the finish line.
  • Complete two clean laps.
  • Change speed with an If condition.

3Two sites, one engine

  • Circuit: Follow Track, Turn, and Drive.
  • Curl ramp: Launch.
  • One physics engine makes results visible.
